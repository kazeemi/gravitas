EXECUTIVE PRESENCE COACHING PLATFORM
Scoring Methodology v3.0
================================================================================

OVERVIEW
--------
Every session is evaluated by AI that listens to your actual audio (and watches
your video frames, if applicable). Scores are based entirely on observable
signals — never inferred from context, assumed from your bio, or guessed from
the topic you chose. The transcript is used only as a secondary reference;
all primary scoring comes from the audio and video analysis directly.

Sessions produce scores on either 6 dimensions (audio-only) or 10 dimensions
(video). A composite score is then computed from the dimension scores.

================================================================================
SCORING TIERS
================================================================================

  Score 1–3   |  Needs Focus    — Absent, severely deficient, or actively
               |                   harmful to your presence.
  Score 4–5   |  Developing     — Some evidence of the skill, but significant
               |                   inconsistency, gaps, or missed opportunities.
  Score 6–7   |  Strong         — Solid, consistent demonstration with only
               |                   minor gaps. Meets expectations for a
               |                   professional speaker.
  Score 8–9   |  Distinguished  — Noticeably above average. Few coaches would
               |                   find fault. Impressive control and
               |                   intentionality.
  Score 10    |  Distinguished  — Exceptionally rare. Reserved for world-class
               |                   delivery. Almost never awarded.

================================================================================
COMPOSITE SCORE
================================================================================

The composite score is the simple average of all dimension scores for the
session, subject to one gating rule:

GATING RULE: If any of the following "anchor" dimensions scores 1–3, the
composite is capped at 8.0, regardless of how well other dimensions scored.
Anchor dimensions: Vocal Clarity, Confidence Language, Structure,
Presence & Engagement (video only).

Rationale: these dimensions reflect foundational presence. A speaker cannot be
Distinguished overall if their core vocal, structural, or physical presence is
fundamentally weak, even if individual delivery mechanics are polished.

================================================================================
AUDIO DIMENSIONS (present in both audio and video sessions)
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. VOCAL CLARITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What is measured:
  Articulation, diction, word-ending clarity, ease of comprehension, absence
  of mumbling. Does every word land clearly without the listener needing to
  strain or re-play?

Data source: Audio delivery analysis — "clarity" field only.

Score guide:
  1   Almost incomprehensible. Heavy mumbling, dropped words, severely muffled
      or slurred speech. The listener cannot follow the content.
  2   Frequently unclear. Multiple words per sentence are missed. Dropped
      endings are constant. Comprehension requires significant effort.
  3   Often unclear. Intermittent mumbling, frequent dropped endings, or a
      persistent articulation issue that degrades comprehension noticeably.
  4   Somewhat unclear. Articulation problems appear regularly, particularly
      under pressure or when speaking quickly. The listener catches most words
      but must fill in gaps.
  5   Mildly unclear. Occasional mumbling or dropped endings, typically in
      faster passages. Generally comprehensible but with noticeable slippage.
  6   Mostly clear. Minor articulation lapses only. Listener can follow without
      effort, with the occasional word requiring inference.
  7   Clear throughout. All words land. Only very subtle, infrequent softening
      at sentence ends.
  8   Crisp and precise. Every word is deliberate and fully formed. Articulation
      is an asset, not a neutral.
  9   Exceptional diction. The voice is a precision instrument. Each consonant
      and vowel is intentional. No drop-off at speed or volume changes.
  10  Broadcast-standard enunciation. Effortless to comprehend. A benchmark for
      other speakers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PACE, RHYTHM & VOCAL VARIETY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What is measured:
  Speaking speed (words per minute), rhythm (steady vs. erratic delivery),
  use of deliberate pauses, pitch variation, intonation, and whether the
  overall vocal delivery is dynamic or monotone.

Data source: Audio delivery analysis — "pace", "pitchIntonation",
"pitchVariationScore", and "silences" fields.

Note on WPM: Pace is calculated from the first spoken word to the last spoken
word. Silence before you start or after you finish is excluded.
Ideal pace: 120–160 words per minute. Slower can be deliberate; faster
risks losing the listener.

Score guide:
  1   Severely dysfunctional pace. Either so fast the content is incomprehensible
      or so slow it is unlistenable. Completely monotone. No pauses of any kind,
      or constant uncontrolled pauses.
  2   Pace is a significant problem. Either consistently too fast (180+ wpm with
      no breathing room) or too slow (under 70 wpm, grinding). Minimal or no
      pitch variation. Rhythm is erratic.
  3   Noticeable pace issues throughout. Frequent rushes or drags. Almost no
      intentional pauses. Pitch is mostly flat with rare variation.
  4   Pace is inconsistent — some sections work, others rush or slow
      uncomfortably. Pauses are rare or ill-placed. Some pitch variation but
      not patterned.
  5   Pace is generally acceptable but with a recurring problem (e.g., speeds up
      under pressure, or a consistent slight drag). Some intentional pauses.
      Pitch varies occasionally.
  6   Solid pace control. Generally in the 120–160 wpm range. Deliberate pauses
      appear. Pitch varies to signal structure and emphasis.
  7   Good pace discipline. The speed serves clarity. Pauses are used
      purposefully. Pitch variation is natural and consistent.
  8   Excellent pace and rhythm. The pace modulates intentionally — slowing for
      key points, moving faster through context. Pauses land where they should.
      Vocal dynamics are a clear tool.
  9   Master-level vocal variety. The listener is guided by pace and pitch.
      Every shift in speed or tone is purposeful and effective. Pauses are
      commanding.
  10  World-class vocal control. The voice alone holds attention. Pace, rhythm,
      and pitch work as a unified instrument of persuasion.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. VOLUME & PROJECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What is measured:
  Audibility, consistency of volume, projection energy, and whether volume
  changes are controlled or erratic.

Data source: Audio delivery analysis — "volume" field only.

Score guide:
  1   Inaudible or distressingly loud. Volume makes comprehension impossible.
  2   Severely under-projected or so loud it creates discomfort. The listener
      cannot easily follow.
  3   Frequently too quiet (trailing off, whispering passages) or inconsistently
      loud in a way that distracts. Projection is not sustained.
  4   Volume is a noticeable issue. Often trails off at sentence ends.
      Projection drops under pressure. Inconsistency is the norm.
  5   Mostly audible but with a recurring dip — typically at sentence endings
      or in longer phrases. Projection is adequate but not commanding.
  6   Consistently audible. Projection holds through sentences. Minor drops only.
  7   Good projection throughout. Volume is controlled and sustains authority.
      Very occasional softening only.
  8   Strong, confident projection. Volume is an active tool — the speaker can
      quiet intentionally for emphasis, then project fully. No unplanned drops.
  9   Exceptional command of volume. Every word carries. Dynamic range is
      controlled and deliberate. The voice fills the space.
  10  Outstanding projection. The speaker's voice commands full attention without
      strain. A model of vocal authority.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. FILLER WORDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What is measured:
  Frequency of filler words and sounds heard in the audio, including: um, uh,
  like, you know, so, basically, literally, actually, right, I mean, kind of,
  sort of, you see.

Data source: Audio delivery analysis — "fillerWords" field only. The audio
model's count is authoritative.

Score guide:
  1   Fillers are pervasive — more than 1 per every 20 words on average.
      The fillers are the dominant feature of the delivery. Almost every pause
      is filled.
  2   Very frequent — roughly 1 per 30 words. Multiple filler types. The
      listener notices them constantly.
  3   Frequent — roughly 1 per 40 words, or a strong habitual filler that
      repeats many times and dominates the listener's attention.
  4   Noticeable — roughly 1 per 60 words. Multiple types observed. The fillers
      are a clear pattern.
  5   Moderate — roughly 1 per 80 words. One or two recurring types. Noticeable
      but not dominant.
  6   Occasional — roughly 1 per 100 words. Fillers appear but don't define the
      delivery.
  7   Infrequent — roughly 1 per 150 words. Only one type, appearing in moments
      of transition or under pressure.
  8   Rare — 1–2 total instances in a typical 60–90 second session. Nearly
      invisible to the listener.
  9   Near-absent — 0–1 instances. Any filler that appears was isolated and
      does not create a pattern.
  10  Zero fillers heard. Complete command of silence. The speaker lets pauses
      land cleanly without filling them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. STRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What is measured:
  Whether the response has a clear, confident opening; an organized and logical
  body; and a decisive close. Whether the prompt was directly addressed.
  Whether the speaker wandered, rambled, or trailed off.

Data source: Audio delivery analysis — "structureObservation" field only.
This is assessed from listening, not from reading the transcript.

RECITATION NOTE: If the session prompt indicates the speaker was reading or
reciting a pre-written text (poem, speech, passage, etc.), this dimension
evaluates how the delivery honored the source text's structure — pacing
through stanzas, pausing at natural breaks, serving the emotional arc —
not whether the speaker constructed original architecture.

Score guide:
  1   No discernible structure. The speaker jumps between topics without
      connection. No opening, no close. The response does not address the
      prompt.
  2   Severely disorganized. One or two points appear but they are not connected
      or sequenced. The prompt is barely addressed. No functional opening
      or close.
  3   Minimal structure. A vague beginning exists but the body wanders with
      little logic. The close is absent or trails off. The prompt is addressed
      only partially.
  4   Some structure visible but inconsistent. A functional opening may exist
      without a clear body plan, or the body is organized but the close is weak.
      The speaker starts well but loses the thread.
  5   Moderate structure. There is a recognizable opening, a somewhat organized
      middle, and an attempt at a close. Significant gaps or wanders remain.
      The prompt is addressed but not sharply.
  6   Solid structure. Clear opening that addresses the prompt directly.
      Organized body with a logical flow. A close that brings it together.
      Minor gaps only.
  7   Well-structured throughout. The response has architecture. Opening,
      transitions, and close work together. Points land in a logical order.
  8   Strong, intentional structure. The speaker has clearly planned how to
      frame the response. The opening hooks immediately, the body flows
      without redundancy, the close is decisive.
  9   Exceptional structure. The response feels like it was designed, not
      improvised. The listener always knows where they are and where they're
      going. Every transition is earned.
  10  Masterful architecture. The structure itself communicates authority.
      Opening, body, and close form a seamless, persuasive whole.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. CONFIDENCE LANGUAGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What is measured:
  The balance of assertive language vs. hedging language as heard in the audio.
  Assertive: "I know", "We will", "The key point is", clear declarative
  statements. Hedging: "I think", "maybe", "I guess", "kind of", "I believe",
  "hopefully", "I'm not sure but".

Data source: Audio delivery analysis — "confidenceLanguageObservation" field.
Specific phrases heard are quoted and assessed.

RECITATION NOTE: If the speaker was reading or reciting a pre-written text,
this dimension evaluates only the vocal conviction and commitment with which
the words were performed — not the word choices themselves.

Score guide:
  1   Language is actively undermining. Nearly every statement is hedged or
      qualified. The speaker sounds unsure of every claim they make.
  2   Pervasive hedging. Hedging phrases dominate. Any assertive language is
      rare and overshadowed. Authority is absent.
  3   Frequent hedging. Multiple types of hedge words heard throughout. The
      speaker consistently softens their positions.
  4   More hedging than assertive. Hedging patterns appear regularly.
      Some assertive phrases exist but the overall impression is tentative.
  5   Mixed. Hedging and assertive language appear roughly equally. Some
      moments of authority, undermined by qualification elsewhere.
  6   Mostly assertive. Hedges appear occasionally but the overall impression
      is one of confidence. Declarative statements dominate.
  7   Predominantly assertive. Hedging is rare. The speaker takes clear
      positions. Language projects authority consistently.
  8   Highly assertive. Clear, direct declarations throughout. Any residual
      hedge is isolated and does not define the pattern.
  9   Exceptional command of language authority. The speaker states positions
      with conviction. Word choices project certainty and ownership.
  10  World-class language of authority. Every sentence conveys mastery and
      confidence. A model of executive communication.

================================================================================
VIDEO DIMENSIONS (video sessions only — assessed from video frames)
================================================================================

Video frames are captured every 8 seconds (first frame at 2.5 seconds), up to
10 frames per session. All visual dimensions are assessed ONLY from what is
directly observed in these frames. If frames are unavailable, visual dimensions
cannot be scored.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. EYE CONTACT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What is measured:
  Where the speaker was looking in each frame. Frequency of direct camera
  connection. Consistency of gaze across the session.

Data source: Video frame analysis — "eyeContactObservation" field only.

Score guide:
  1   The speaker is never looking at or near the camera. Eyes are consistently
      cast down, sideways, or away.
  2   Camera connection is nearly absent. Rare glances toward the camera but
      no sustained eye contact across frames.
  3   Occasional camera connection in one or two frames. The speaker frequently
      looks away. Consistency is absent.
  4   Some camera connection but unreliable. Several frames show good gaze;
      others show the speaker looking away. The listener would notice.
  5   Moderate eye contact. The speaker connects with the camera more often
      than not, but breaks are frequent enough to weaken the effect.
  6   Good eye contact. Consistent camera connection in most frames. Breaks are
      infrequent and don't disrupt the sense of connection.
  7   Strong eye contact. The speaker maintains direct camera connection through
      nearly all frames. Breaks are rare and brief.
  8   Excellent eye contact. Consistent, warm, direct camera connection
      throughout. The viewer feels spoken to.
  9   Exceptional. The gaze is deliberate and commanding. Camera connection is
      sustained and purposeful in every frame.
  10  World-class eye contact. The speaker's gaze communicates full presence and
      authority. A benchmark for video delivery.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. GESTURE & MOVEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What is measured:
  Hand and arm gestures observed in the frames. Whether gestures are purposeful
  and reinforce the message, absent, or distracting.

Data source: Video frame analysis — "gestureObservation" field only.

Score guide:
  1   Gestures are actively harmful — erratic, distracting, or nervous
      movements that undermine presence.
  2   Gestures are mostly distracting. Fidgeting, self-touching, or constant
      movement that draws attention away from the message.
  3   Either very sparse and rigid (no expressive movement at all), or gesture
      patterns that consistently distract.
  4   Gestures are present but stiff or mechanical. Some purposeful movement
      but it lacks naturalness.
  5   Some purposeful gesturing that supports the message, with some awkward or
      absent moments. Movement is uneven.
  6   Good use of gesture. Movements generally support and reinforce the
      content. Minor stiffness or missed opportunities only.
  7   Natural, purposeful gesturing. The hands work with the message. Movements
      feel unscripted and appropriate.
  8   Excellent gesture work. The hands are an active communication tool.
      Movements are expressive, timed well, and reinforce key points.
  9   Exceptional. Gesture and movement are fully integrated with speech.
      The physical delivery amplifies the message at every turn.
  10  World-class physical expressiveness. Gesture and movement are seamless
      and powerful.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. PRESENCE & ENGAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What is measured:
  Physical and vocal energy together — posture, body language, visible
  engagement, forward lean or pull-back, and the overall energy the speaker
  projects.

Data source: Video frame "presenceObservation" for physical presence, plus
audio "energy" and "confidence" fields for vocal energy.

Score guide:
  1   No visible presence or energy. The speaker appears disengaged,
      slumped, or disconnected.
  2   Very low energy. Posture is collapsed or static. Vocal energy is flat.
      The speaker does not appear to be present in the conversation.
  3   Minimal presence. Some physical engagement but largely passive. Energy is
      below what the context requires.
  4   Limited presence. Either the body or voice conveys some energy, but not
      both. The speaker is present but not commanding.
  5   Moderate presence. Adequate engagement with the occasional moment of real
      energy. Does not consistently hold attention through presence alone.
  6   Solid presence. Good posture, reasonable physical engagement, and
      consistent vocal energy. The speaker clearly cares.
  7   Strong presence. Both physical and vocal energy work together. The speaker
      draws attention and holds it.
  8   Excellent presence. The speaker is fully "on." Physical and vocal energy
      are aligned and elevated. The session has command.
  9   Exceptional presence. Rare quality of engagement. The speaker fills the
      frame energetically and vocally. Hard to look away.
  10  World-class executive presence. The speaker commands attention with every
      dimension of their delivery simultaneously.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. PROFESSIONAL APPEARANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What is measured:
  Attire, grooming, and background as visible in the video frames. Whether the
  speaker's appearance supports or undermines the professional context.

Data source: Video frame analysis — "professionalAppearanceObservation" only.

Score guide:
  1   Appearance actively undermines professional context — inappropriate
      attire, significantly disheveled, or background is chaotic and
      distracting.
  2   Significant appearance concerns. Attire, grooming, or background would
      create a strong negative impression in a professional setting.
  3   Below professional standard. Attire or background is noticeably
      inappropriate or unprepared for the context.
  4   Somewhat professional but with clear gaps — e.g., casual attire in a
      formal context, or a cluttered/distracting background.
  5   Mostly professional with one or two issues — e.g., appropriate clothing
      but unkempt, or acceptable attire with a suboptimal background.
  6   Professional standard met. Attire and grooming are appropriate. Background
      is neutral or professional.
  7   Clearly professional. Well put together. Background is clean and
      appropriate. Overall appearance supports credibility.
  8   Strongly professional. Polished attire, neat grooming, and a deliberately
      set-up, clean background. Appearance is an asset.
  9   Exceptional presentation. Every visual element is intentional and
      reinforces executive credibility.
  10  Impeccable. Appearance and environment convey the highest level of
      professional polish.

================================================================================
DATA SOURCES — STRICT SEPARATION
================================================================================

The platform enforces that each dimension is scored only from its designated
data source. This prevents the AI from guessing or inferring:

  AUDIO dimensions   →  Scored from gpt-audio's analysis of the actual
  (all 6)               recording. Never from the transcript. Never from
                         the video.

  VISUAL dimensions  →  Scored from Claude Vision's analysis of captured
  (video only, 4)       video frames. Never from audio. Never inferred
                         from context or recording setup.

If a data source is unavailable (e.g., no video frames were captured),
the affected dimensions explicitly state they could not be assessed.

================================================================================
RECITATION & READING CONTEXT
================================================================================

When the session prompt indicates the speaker was reading or reciting a
pre-written text (poem, speech, passage, excerpt, prayer, monologue, etc.),
two dimensions are evaluated differently:

  STRUCTURE:           Evaluates how the delivery honored the source text's
                       structure through pacing, pausing, and phrasing —
                       NOT whether the speaker constructed original
                       architecture.

  CONFIDENCE LANGUAGE: Evaluates the vocal conviction and commitment with
                       which the words were performed — NOT the word choices
                       themselves (which belong to the source text).

================================================================================
ACCURACY & CALIBRATION NOTES
================================================================================

- Scores 9 and 10 are rare. A 9 means the AI found almost nothing to improve.
  A 10 is reserved for world-class delivery rarely encountered even among
  professional speakers.

- The platform is calibrated to be rigorous. A 7 is a genuinely good score.
  A 6 means solid professional standard. Scores of 5 indicate room for
  meaningful growth.

- Composite scores between 6.0 and 7.9 are Strong overall — a real
  accomplishment. Distinguished (8.0+) requires sustained excellence across
  the majority of dimensions.

- WPM is calculated from the first spoken word to the last spoken word.
  Any silence before you start or after you finish is excluded.

- The platform does not store your audio or video recordings. Processing
  happens in memory and your raw recording is discarded after analysis.
