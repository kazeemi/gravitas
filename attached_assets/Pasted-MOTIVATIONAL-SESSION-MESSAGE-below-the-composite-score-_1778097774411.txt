MOTIVATIONAL SESSION MESSAGE — below the composite score, above the summary sections
Every report includes a short motivational message placed directly below the composite score and tier label, above the "What's working well / What's worth working on" sections. This message is always visible, never collapsed.
Rules for generating this message:
It is always about the act of showing up — never about the score itself. The user should feel seen as a person who is investing in themselves, regardless of how the session went.
It is one sentence. Two sentences maximum only if the second adds meaningfully. It must fit within two lines on screen — never longer.
The tone is enthusiastic and celebratory by default. However, if the composite score has dropped across two or more consecutive sessions, or if the overall composite is in the Needs Focus tier, shift to quietly affirming rather than loudly celebratory. Acknowledge the effort, not the outcome.
Session-specific logic:
Session 1: Acknowledge that starting is the hardest part. The message should feel like a warm welcome and a genuine recognition that they have taken a real first step.
Session 2: Acknowledge that returning matters more than most people realise. The fact that they came back signals something about their commitment.
Session 3 and beyond: Stop counting sessions. Focus on the pattern — consistency, commitment, the habit of self-development. Reference the journey, not the number.
Score comparison logic — from session 2 onwards:
If the composite score has improved by 0.5 or more since the last session: acknowledge the progress implicitly, optionally naming the pillar or dimension that moved most. Do not state the numbers — say "your vocal delivery has shifted noticeably" not "your score went from 6.2 to 7.1."
If the composite score has improved by less than 0.5: acknowledge the return without referencing score movement. Focus entirely on showing up.
If the composite score has stayed the same or dropped: do not reference the score at all. Praise the return warmly and frame consistency as the work. Something like: "Coming back is the practice — and you keep showing up." Never make the user feel they have failed by returning with a lower score.
Tone guidance with examples — these are illustrations, not templates:
Session 1, any score: "This is where it starts — and starting takes more courage than most people give themselves credit for."
Session 2, score improved: "You came back — and your vocal presence is already showing it."
Session 2, score same or dropped: "Two sessions in. Most people never get here — you did."
Session 3+, score improved noticeably: "The consistency is doing its work — your thinking clarity in particular has shifted."
Session 3+, score flat or dropped: "Showing up when it is hard is exactly the work. You are doing it."
Session 3+, strong sustained performance: "You keep coming back and it keeps showing — this is what development actually looks like."
Never use: hollow corporate phrases like "great job", "well done", "amazing effort", "you're crushing it", or anything that sounds automated. The message should feel like it was written by a coach who knows this person — not generated by a system trying to sound encouraging
